## 3. Saturating power-law fit per arm

`AUROC(n) = ceiling - a * n^(-b)`. `ceiling` is the fitted asymptote (the irreducible-error bound for that representation), `b` the scaling exponent.

| C | excl. 10k | not identifiable | not identifiable | 0.8063 | - |

- **Arm A is the only arm with an identifiable ceiling.** Its fitted asymptote sits just above where it already is, i.e. engineered features are close to saturation on this task: more patients buy very little.
- No ceiling is identifiable for B (excl. 10k), C (excl. 10k). Over the measured range these arms are still on the near-linear part of the curve, so the data cannot distinguish a high asymptote reached slowly from a lower one reached later. This is reported as a limitation rather than papered over with an extrapolated number: it is the same fact that makes the parity estimates in section 4 fragile.

| A vs B | >= 100k | -0.0253 | 2.35e+07 | 1.32 |

- **A vs C: parity estimates span 5.90e+06 to 7.79e+06**, a 1.3x spread driven purely by which rungs enter the fit.
- **A vs B: parity estimates span 2.35e+07 to 7.36e+07**, a 3.1x spread driven purely by which rungs enter the fit.

**How to state this.** A single extrapolated crossover point is not defensible from six rungs whose lowest points are optimisation-limited. The claim the data supports is a bound: under log-linear gap closure, parity between engineered features and a learned sequence representation requires on the order of 10^7 patients or more, one to two orders of magnitude beyond the 1.1M rung measured here, and beyond the size of any single commercial claims cohort in routine use. Engineered features remain ahead throughout the achievable range.

- The Transformer does **not** dominate the 2017 CNN at every scale. It is behind at 10,000 and first overtakes at **30,000** training patients. Reporting 'a modern Transformer beats the older CNN' without this qualifier is not supported by the ladder.
- Part of the deficit at the smallest rung is the optimisation failure documented in section 2, so the honest reading is that the Transformer needs both more data and a more forgiving training budget before its architecture pays off.
