# Paired bootstrap confidence intervals: arthritis onset

_Generated by `reports/paired_bootstrap.py`. 2,000 bootstrap resamples of the 137,689 held-out patients, stratified to preserve 2,554 incident cases. Arms are compared **paired on the same patients**. Each arm's value in a resample is the **mean over its 3 seeds of the per-seed metric**, which is what the learning-curve tables report, so these intervals attach to the numbers used elsewhere._

## Why these intervals and not the seed SD

At the top rung, Arm A's AUROC has a DeLong standard error of **0.0036** (sampling error of the metric on 2,554 cases), while its spread over 3 training seeds is **0.0005**, roughly 7x smaller. The seed SD answers 'how much does retraining move this?', not 'how precisely do we know it?'. Reporting it as the error bar would overstate precision by an order of magnitude.

The *paired* difference between two arms is much better determined than either arm's absolute AUROC, because patient-level noise is shared and cancels. That is why the gap intervals below are far tighter than the individual standard errors.

## Reading it

- At 1,114,144 training patients, engineered features lead the Transformer by **0.0216 AUROC (95% CI 0.0183 to 0.0247)**. The interval excludes zero, so the lead is distinguishable from noise on this test set.
- On AUPRC the same gap is **0.0116 (95% CI 0.0070 to 0.0164)**, against a base rate of 0.0185.
- The Transformer's lead over the 2017 CNN at the top rung is **+0.0143 (95% CI +0.0113 to +0.0172)**, which excludes zero.

## Single model versus a 3-seed ensemble

The table above is the expected performance of **one** trained model. Averaging the three seeds' predictions instead gives a small ensemble, and that changes the gaps, because the noisier arms gain more from averaging:

| comparison | single model (mean over seeds) | 3-seed ensemble | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| A - C at 1,114,144 | +0.0216 | +0.0158 | -0.0058 |
| A - B at 1,114,144 | +0.0358 | +0.0277 | -0.0082 |
| C - B at 1,114,144 | +0.0143 | +0.0119 | -0.0024 |

- Ensembling narrows the features-vs-Transformer gap by **0.0058 AUROC**, which says a real part of the Transformer's deficit at this scale is **variance, not bias**: it is not that the representation cannot capture the signal, it is that a single training run lands inconsistently. That is a cheap and honest observation to make, and it points at training budget rather than architecture as the binding constraint.
- The paper reports the single-model numbers as primary, since that is what the learning curves and the scaling fits use.

_Caveat: these intervals quantify sampling error on one fixed test set from one insurer population. They do not cover the design asymmetries (sequence truncation, fixed model capacity across rungs) documented in the limitations, which are larger than the intervals shown here._
