## 2. Index-year composition and the enrollment boundary

Index date is the patient's first enrolled month plus 12, so index year is set by when their enrollment span starts. Enrollment data begins at 201901, so patients already enrolled when the data window opens all receive `FIRST_MONTH = 201901` and land in index year 2020. This is a left-truncation artifact and is disclosed here because it makes the train and test sets differ in enrollment tenure.

| index year | split | N | % of split | incident | onset % | mean age | % at 201901 boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 | train | 689,686 | 61.9% | 13,509 | 1.96 | 45.9 | 86.6% |
| 2021 | train | 182,742 | 16.4% | 3,827 | 2.09 | 43.6 | 0.0% |
| 2022 | train | 241,716 | 21.7% | 4,044 | 1.67 | 40.9 | 0.0% |
| 2023 | test | 137,689 | 100.0% | 2,554 | 1.85 | 44.3 | 0.0% |

- **597,065 (47.7%)** of the cohort sits exactly at the 201901 enrollment boundary. All of them fall in index year 2020, which is why that year dominates the train pool.
- The test set (index 2023) contains **no** boundary patients: every member joined during 2022. Train and test therefore differ in enrollment tenure, not only in time.
- Baseline windows are calendar 2019 for index-2020 patients and calendar 2022 for the test set, so the outcome window of the largest training stratum overlaps the 2020 care disruption.
- The shift is not only temporal: region mix moves by up to **7.0 percentage points** (largest: W, 20.0% of train vs 27.0% of test), and the 50-64 age band is 3.1 points lighter in test. Any arm that is more sensitive to covariate shift is penalised by this, which is a competing explanation for the arm ranking and is treated as such.

## 3. Baseline-window observability (feeds the fairness discussion)

| split | label | N | % empty baseline | % truncated at 200 tokens | mean tokens | median |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| test | 0 | 135,135 | 15.7% | 17.2% | 76.7 | 50 |
| test | 1 | 2,554 | 3.3% | 40.1% | 130.1 | 151 |
| train | 0 | 1,092,764 | 15.9% | 16.8% | 75.6 | 49 |
| train | 1 | 21,380 | 5.9% | 36.9% | 123.8 | 136 |

- **Truncation is differential by outcome.** Sequence models (Arms B and C) see at most the most-recent 200 tokens; the engineered-feature arm (Arm A) aggregates the full window with no cap. Incident cases are truncated at roughly twice the rate of non-cases, so the sequence arms receive a censored view of the positive class. This is an asymmetry in the direction of Arm A and is reported as a limitation.
- Patients with an empty baseline window are retained with demographics only. They are far more common among non-cases, so they are close to free negatives for every arm.
